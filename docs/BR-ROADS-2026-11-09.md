| Doubled roads (two roads within 9 m for over 60 m) | 66 |
| Roads through water | 32 |

**Visible problems:**

- Tangles of small loops, triangles and doubled stretches where the tracer followed the art's strokes. They were worst round the hospital, the quarry, the military base and the airfield.
- Spurs that end in a field.
- Roads stopping a few metres short of the road they meet.
- Murder Cabins had no road at all.

### The finish pass (`build_world.py finish_network`)

It runs after the network is joined, in this order:

1. **Water:** a road in water off a bridge is cut there.
2. **Bridge ends:** each bridge end is linked to the nearest road ahead of it, up to 450 m away, over dry ground.
3. **Junction gaps:** an end 3-24 m short of another road is closed. An end that overshoots a road is cut back to it.
4. **Hairpins:** kinks over 150° are removed.
5. **Dead ends:** each loose end (outside towns and site pads, not at a bridge) is linked to another road up to 800 m away, never back into its own branch. The link runs over dry ground no steeper than 18 %, and never through a town or a pad.
6. **Tangles:** the network is taken junction to junction. Of each country loop shorter than 1 km, and each lasso shorter than 450 m, the longest link goes, longest first. Doubled stretches lose the longer copy. A link is only removed between two real junctions, so no road is cut in two, and the towns, the Superstore and the site pads are left alone.
7. **Spurs:** a dead end up to 300 m back to its junction goes. Roads between junctions are never touched.
8. **Cut-off pieces:** any piece not connected to the main network is joined to it by the cheapest route over the ground, using a least-cost path on a 15.6 m grid. The cost is the slope; water, towns and pads cannot be crossed.
9. **Site access:** a site with no road within reach gets one, routed the same way. This gave Murder Cabins a road to the summer camp. The mountain, Iron Peaks, stays roadless: it is climbed.

**Two bugs found on the way, both fixed:**

- **Junctions coming apart:** a junction can be a small cluster of points (roads within 4 m of each other). With some of its roads removed, the others no longer met. Rebuilt roads now end at the junction's centre.
- **Short connectors dropped:** connectors under 12 m were dropped, which disconnected the network in places. They are kept now when they link two junctions.

### Grading

- **Limits:** cuttings may now go to 24 m and fills to 10 m (6 m before). A road over a ridge goes through a pass at the 13 % grade, instead of climbing at 40 % where the cut ran out.
- **Level joins:** roads are graded longest first. A side road is held to the height of the road it joins, and may cut or fill up to 8 m more within 150 m of the join, so it climbs to the junction at the grade.
- **Lakes:** a road within about 45 m of a lake never goes below the water. A cutting through lake-3's raised bank had left the lake's surface 12 m above the road beside it.

## The map's edges and the terrain joins

A new terrain audit (`terrain_audit.py`, in the session scratchpad) checks:

- land at the map's border;
- water below sea level cut off from the sea;
- river channels running dry;
- lake shores: dry holes, and water standing above lower ground;
- cliffs and coastal walls;
- land cover against height;
- bridge ends.

| Finding | main | now |
|---|---|---|
| Land at the map's edge | 27 headlands, up to 17 m high | none |
| Lake shores: water standing over lower ground | n/a (the lakes were dry) | 0 |
| Lake shores: dry holes below the water | n/a | 1 (7 cells, lake-13 by the south coast) |
| Cliffs outside the rock areas | 9 | 6 (4 of them under lake-3's water) |

### The sea round the whole map

The concept's land ran off the map on all four sides. The terrain stops at the map's edge, so those were sheer walls with nothing beneath.

- **The shore:** the land now ends 120-300 m inside the edge, on a wavy line, and the usual beach and sea floor follow.
- **Under Iron Peaks:** the sea comes in only about 100 m, and the massif comes down to it no steeper than 1.4:1, a rocky sea cliff. The main summit and its radio mast are untouched. The western shoulder's perch is a little lower. A steeper face would break the terrain LOD's range rule. It cost a few coastal roads near the edge: 58 roads were cut back from the new water. Also, two river crossings at the east edge no longer have a bank to stand on.

### Lake shores

- **Cover follows the water:** a lake's water now covers its whole basin (everything below its level that the lake reaches). A cover texel is water if any height cell under it is. On a steep shore, the texel at the shore's foot is water too.
- **Why:** before, a 4-8 m strip along lake-3's steep north shore showed a dry trench 12 m deep. The steep-ground rock cover had also been taking those texels back.
- **Coastal lakes:** the lake banks are now raised after the beaches, which had flattened the banks of the lakes near the coast back below their water. Within 40 m of the sea they ease down to the beach instead.
- **Basin fill:** a basin fills only within about 30 m of the lake's outline, so a coastal lake does not spill over the low land between it and the sea.

## Roadside: ditches and cover

**Ditches:** beyond each shoulder of every country road there is a ditch, 1.2 m deep along a flat middle and 7 m wide (5.5-12.5 m from the centreline), deep enough to crouch or lie in out of sight of the road. A narrower ditch did not survive the 3.9 m height grid. There are none:

- in the towns or the Superstore's ring;
- on site pads;
- within 40 m of a bridge or dam;
- within about 45 m of water.

Signs and power poles now stand 13.5 m out and stone walls 14.5 m out, past the ditch.

**Road surface:** the asphalt ribbon's edges are now kept at least as high as the centreline. Next to a ditch, the height grid pulled the edge samples down, and the ribbon sagged below the crown, so a strip of grass showed down the middle.

**Cover along the country roads** (`layout.ts roadsideCover`): every 550-950 m, outside the sites, one of:

- a dry stone wall, 21-35 m long, beyond the ditch;
- one or two wrecks on the verge (the island's roads already had their own);
- an abandoned roadblock: two jersey barriers across one lane, a wooden barricade behind them, roadwork posts leading up to it, and a wreck slewed across the other lane;
- hay bales at a field's edge.

**Guardrails:** where a road runs on an embankment (the ground 13 m out lies 1.8 m or more below the road), runs of three or more 10.8 m sections go on the shoulder.

**Lamps:**

- a double-arm lamp at each country junction, past both roads' ditches;
- on the roads into the towns, a lamp every 45 m from 60 to 420 m out.

Lamps only stand where the ground under their arms is level to within 1.2 m.

**Power lines:** about one pole in seven carries a transformer. The wires run across both pole types.

**Signs:** the roadside signs now mix speed-limit, curve, deer-crossing and campground signs (campground and deer in the forests) with the old sign.

Walls, roadblocks and guardrails only go where the road runs within 10-20° of north-south or east-west, because placements turn in quarter turns.
